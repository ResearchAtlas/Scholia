# Screening

Adapted from K-Dense's literature-review skill (MIT), `references/core_workflow.md`, phases 1 and 3; see NOTICE.md.

## Criteria first

Write the inclusion and exclusion criteria before reading any paper closely:
- the population, setting or data the question concerns;
- the designs that count (for example randomized trials, observational studies, models, qualitative studies);
- the period and languages, when they matter;
- the outcomes or results that would answer the question.

For a clinical or intervention question, the population, intervention, comparison and outcome help make the question specific.

## Stages

1. **Duplicates.** The same work can be in the library twice, or as a preprint and a journal article. Treat reports of one study as one study, and say which report you used.
2. **Titles.** Exclude papers that are plainly outside the question. Count them.
3. **Abstracts or opening passages.** Apply the criteria. Record a short reason for each exclusion.
4. **Full text.** Read what decides eligibility. Record the reason for each exclusion and the final number included.

Keep the counts at each stage so they add up: listed, excluded at each stage with reasons, included. Never round them or invent them.

## Limits to state

- The review covers only the papers in the library.
- One reader screened; a systematic review would use two for the final decisions.
- A record that is only an abstract or metadata could not be assessed in full.

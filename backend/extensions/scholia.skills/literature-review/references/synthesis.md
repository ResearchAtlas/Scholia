# Synthesis

Adapted from K-Dense's literature-review skill (MIT), `references/core_workflow.md`, phases 4 and 5, and its best practices; see NOTICE.md.

## Extraction

For each included paper, note:
- its design and methods;
- its sample, population or data;
- its main findings, with the conditions under which it reports them;
- the limitations it states;
- anything that bears on its risk of bias, using the appraisal suited to its design, without forcing every design onto one scale.

## Themes

- Identify a few major themes across the papers; a paper may belong to more than one.
- Within each theme, synthesize across papers: compare approaches and results, mark consensus and controversy, and highlight the strongest evidence.
- Do not write one paragraph per paper.

## Critical analysis

- Weigh the methods' strengths and limitations across papers, and how consistent the evidence is.
- Name the gaps: questions, populations or methods no included paper addresses.
- Note possible publication bias where only positive results appear.

## Writing

- Be objective and specific: give numbers and conditions where the papers report them, each cited.
- Organize under clear headings by theme.
- End with the review's own limits and directions for further work.
